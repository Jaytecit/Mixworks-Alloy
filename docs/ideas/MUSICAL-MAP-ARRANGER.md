# Idea: musical-map-based arrangement

Recorded from the user's pasted concept on **20 October 2026**. **Idea only:
not scheduled, not part of the current implementation plan, and not an
instruction to implement or investigate now.** The source's proposed order and
imperatives are retained as part of the idea, not current authorisation.

[The complete original text](MUSICAL-MAP-ARRANGER-original.txt) is preserved
byte-for-byte. Its main proposals are:

- Cache a musical map alongside BPM/key: timing confidence, evidence-based
  phrases/sections, vocal/bass/percussion activity, local tonality,
  loudness/brightness/energy and repeated or distinctive events. Keep observations
  separate from interpretations; allow uncertainty, unknowns and corrections.
- Assess combinations at arrangement time: try alternative cues, overlap lengths
  and treatments against the actual passages. Let track order and transition
  suitability influence one another rather than relying solely on whole-track keys.
- Give transition techniques eligibility rules, adaptable parameters and safe
  fallbacks. Optional effects must justify themselves against doing nothing.
- Apply a whole-set performance policy for restraint/activity, smooth versus
  decisive transitions, track preservation, energy progression and sample use.
  Respect musical space, plan ahead and retain a simple continuous-playback fallback.
- Coordinate purposeful bass/EQ/filter/echo treatments, then consider verified
  loops and conservative tagged samples; stems remain an optional later enhancement.
- Expose editable automation and reasons; evaluate loudness-matched listening
  comparisons and difficult cases alongside numerical timing, clipping and
  discontinuity checks. One decoded-audio timeline must underpin analysis/playback.
