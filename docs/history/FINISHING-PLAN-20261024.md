## How to use the order

Work down the stages, delivering one useful increment at a time. Effort labels are relative: **small** means a local behaviour/presentation adjustment, **medium** crosses a few existing owners, and **large** introduces a substantial workflow, representation or integration. These are not time estimates. A reproduced audio-breakup or data-loss defect takes priority over cosmetic work regardless of stage.

Extend existing source/grid, project/transition, MIDI-profile, workspace, prepared-audio and renderer owners. Do not build a second automation, controller or audio engine. Keep musical edits undoable, workspace preferences outside musical Undo, and playback/export/session interpretation consistent. At implementation time, confirm each substantial feature's producer/consumers and narrow its acceptance cases into a focused change.

**P54 feasibility assessment:** a standalone touch controller is technically feasible in principle. Android documents [USB/BLE/virtual MIDI support](https://source.android.com/docs/core/audio/midi); [WebSocket](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/WebSockets_API) supports bidirectional command/state exchange and is one prototype transport, not a chosen standalone-app framework. Actual OS/device support must be checked, especially for a phone acting as a USB peripheral. Our engineering recommendation is to keep audio on the desktop and send small control/state messages over the local network, comparing a supported wired path for consistency. Total delay includes touch sampling, transport/queues, desktop scheduling and the audio buffer; low or unobtrusive delay may be achievable, but literal zero delay and wireless jitter elimination are not credible promises. Quantising a cue to the next beat/bar can make launches musically aligned, while intentionally adding wait time. This is a proposed architecture pending prototype measurements, not implemented support or a guaranteed latency figure.
