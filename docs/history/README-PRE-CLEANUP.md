A native Windows workstation being reconstructed from the installed MixMeister Fusion 7.7.0.1, using REA evidence. The approved Alloy identity combines graphite surfaces, brushed-metal hexagonal controls and copper accents with DAW editing, VST hosting, and simple Novation FLkey 49 control.

Run **Mixworks.cmd** after building. Releases live under **out/releases/**; the launcher selects the latest deployed build. Earlier `out/current/Mixworks.exe` and `out/Mixworks.exe` locations remain fallback options. This is an initial working reconstruction, not full Fusion feature parity.

The app opens in borderless fullscreen across the full monitor, including the taskbar area: 1920 × 1080 on a 1080p 16:9 display, or the selected monitor's native size with Windows scaling respected. The top-right **Windowed / Fullscreen** button and **F11** switch modes. **Display** or **View → Display** offers fullscreen, maximised (taskbar visible), windowed, fitting 16:9 window sizes and a monitor chooser. Use **Close**, **File → Exit Mixworks** or **Alt+F4** to exit. An unsaved mix offers **Save**, **Discard** and **Cancel**. Save writes the current session (or asks for a filename for a new mix) before exiting; cancelled or failed saves keep the mix open. A clean mix closes immediately. If a background task is active, Save keeps the window open and asks you to wait for that task before saving again; Discard still permits closing.

Roll the mouse wheel over the timeline to zoom in/out around the time beneath the cursor. Hold **Shift** while rolling to scroll tracks vertically; horizontal trackpad gestures still scroll sideways. The +/- zoom buttons keep the viewport centre fixed.

## First session

1. **Import audio**, or drag WAV, AIFF, FLAC, Ogg or MP3 files onto the library.
   A central progress panel shows files processed, elapsed time, the current filename and decoding/analysis/cache stage. Tracks appear in the library as they finish. **Stop import** finishes the current track, keeps completed tracks and skips the remaining queue. Closing the app also stops after the current track instead of processing the entire queue before exit.
2. **Drag a library row onto the timeline** to choose its lane. New audio tracks automatically align their inferred or corrected 4/4 bars to the timeline bars. Unknown bar phase still permits pulse alignment when the beat grid is reliable. Source audio is retained: the first clip may have a short leading gap, and later clips choose the next aligned position at the selected overlap (up to one bar shorter than requested). IN and OUT handles meet at the overlap start. The first track establishes the global tempo. Incoming tracks automatically match the shared tempo. The default transition swaps bass across the overlap, with a one-beat handoff near its midpoint and no added volume fades. When arranging with playback stopped, the global tempo ramps between track targets, reaching the incoming track's native BPM at IN. During playback, additions sync to the running tempo map and leave existing voices, plugin state and transport running. A live drop behind the playhead joins at an upcoming aligned beat/bar. Each added track supplies one point in the bottom **TEMPO** bar; move it to adjust timing, and click its number to change the target BPM. Both tracks follow that map with pitch-preserving audio preparation.
   You can also drop audio files directly from Explorer; multiple files follow one another on the chosen lane. Clips appear immediately while source checks, analysis and tempo matching run in the background. Unknown external-file durations are labelled pending. Source-loading edits and Undo/Redo retain the pending matching operation; incoming clips stay silent until ready, while existing playback continues. Save and file output wait for readiness. A failed source stays visibly unavailable. A tempo preparation failure stays marked for retry with **Beatmatch**.
   Double-click is the automatic append shortcut: clips alternate across four lanes and use the selected transition length, initially **8 bars**. Library and Explorer drops use the selected overlap, initially 8 bars; horizontal drop position does not override this initial placement. Move a placed track with Snap on/off or Alt to choose its final position. Moves made during source loading survive preparation. Choose Cut or a 4/8/16/32-bar overlap and click **Beatmatch** to rebuild the whole arrangement. Earlier tempo points outside a new transition are retained. Tracks without a reliable BPM remain unsynced and are reported for review.
3. Drag clips to move them in time or onto another lane. Matched clips snap their beats to the background grid; nearby beats on a deliberately free-positioned clip also act as snap targets. Turn **Snap** off, or hold **Alt**, to slide freely. Moving preserves the source audio and follows the shared tempo map immediately, without repeating analysis or preparing another whole track. Lane-only and unchanged-tempo moves reuse the prepared audio. Click empty timeline space to seek. Use the clip inspector to change source offset, duration, fades and gain. Split at the playhead with **Split** or **S**.
   **Reverse** plays the selected source section backwards. **Loop...** repeats a 1/2/4/8/16/32-beat section from **Source in**, 2/4/8/16/32 times. Looping retains tempo matching and supports reverse; split edits retain the loop's source phase. Use **Remove loop** to return to one pass of the section. Loop boundaries repeat the source directly without an added seam crossfade.
4. Select an audio track on the timeline, open the right-hand **Effects** tab, click **+ VST3**, and choose an installed 64-bit plugin. Double-click an insert to open its native interface. Inserts process from top to bottom on that audio track, before its lane mixer. Moving between lanes retains its insert instances, parameter state and automation. Overlapping audio tracks on the same lane keep independent effects. Copy/paste and Duplicate clone insert instances. The selected audio track's instrument also receives keyboard MIDI with playback stopped. With no audio track selected, inserts remain available on a mixer lane for live instruments. Bypass and removal are available. The **MIDI** tab contains the on-screen keyboard.
5. **Save** a `.mixworks` session to retain the full imported library (including unused tracks), manual analysis corrections, tempo map, label markers, source alignment markers, transition partners/band settings/locks, captured source clocks, clip placement, looping/reverse, mixer/EQ settings, plugin descriptions and plugin state. Projects reference the original audio paths; keep those files in place. Existing saves receive a `.bak` backup; later backups use a fresh sibling name to preserve previous backups. Reopening reuses valid analysis, decoded audio and tempo-preparation caches, with background rebuilding on a miss. Reopening a malformed project or a project with missing audio/plugins keeps the current session. The current format is version 22 and reads versions 1–21. Older sessions transfer lane inserts, EQ, volume/pan and automation to their audio tracks on reopening. Earlier app releases cannot open saves newer than their supported format.
6. **Export WAV** renders the same mixer and VST3 chain to a stereo 24-bit WAV at the current audio-device sample rate. There is no automatic limiter; watch the master peak meter. Choose last-clip cutoff or include the saved effect-tail window. Progress and Cancel stay responsive; cancellation keeps the destination. Configure the window from Mix → Effect-tail window; see [WAV export](../EXPORTING.md).

Space toggles playback. Ctrl+S saves, Ctrl+O opens, Ctrl+Z undoes arrangement/mixer edits, Ctrl+Shift+Z redoes, Ctrl+D duplicates and Delete removes the selected clip. Plugin operations and plugin parameter changes are outside arrangement undo.

Active VST3 inserts must currently report zero processing latency; full parallel-path delay compensation is unfinished. Unsupported inserts remain editable and savable, with an explicit blocked message. Choose their zero-latency mode or bypass them before playback/export. See [plugin latency](../PLUGIN-LATENCY.md).

The selected audio track's **EQ** button (or a mixer lane with no audio track selected) opens a built-in stereo equalizer: bass shelf at 120 Hz, mid bell at 1 kHz, treble shelf at 8 kHz, with -60 to +12 dB gain. It processes after the associated VST3 insert chain and before its pan/volume controls, in live playback and export. **Reset flat** returns all bands to zero; flat EQ leaves PCM unchanged. EQ changes support arrangement undo. This is a new implementation, not a claim of identical Fusion filter curves.

## Tempo timeline and beat snapping

The compact **TEMPO** strip at the bottom of the timeline controls mix BPM. Small pins and BPM numbers share a fixed baseline, following the MixMeister reference; the strip stays visible when scrolling through tracks and follows horizontal timeline scrolling. Adding the first track creates its tempo target at zero, using its detected or manually corrected BPM. Each additional track creates one target at its IN handle; no future targets are created in advance. Targets ramp between marker positions; Cut uses a step at the incoming boundary. Stable detected BPMs or your manual overrides supply the defaults; uncertain tracks remain unsynced. During playback, a newly added track takes the current mix tempo at its IN so adding it cannot change the running clock; edit its target to choose another BPM.

Tempo markers use small hourglass pins with plain numeric BPM labels. Drag a marker horizontally to move its transition; vertical motion leaves BPM unchanged. Click its displayed number for precise seconds and BPM. Press **P** to add a marker at the playhead, choose **Add Tempo Marker** from the timeline or tempo-strip right-click menu, or double-click an empty part of the strip. Right-click a marker to edit or remove it. The first marker stays at zero. The existing tempo map still ramps between transition targets. Delete removes a selected footer marker; Enter opens its numeric editor. The **MIX BPM** box follows the playhead; selecting a marker changes its label to **TARGET**. Drag that numeric box up/down to adjust by 0.1 BPM per pixel, or double-click its number to type. With no marker selected, changing the box adds a target at the playhead. Click the ruler or a clip to return to the current BPM display.

Ruler beats, waveform beat markers, clip placement and automation-point snapping use the same integrated tempo map, including changing beat spacing through ramps. Moving a synced clip snaps its measured first pulse to the grid rather than assuming its left edge is a beat. Labels and automation always snap to the visible background grid. Unsynced clips still show their own detected beat markers. Snap off or Alt allows exact free placement, including intentional offsets from the other tracks.

Moving across a different part of a tempo ramp uses continuous pitch-preserving playback from the already decoded source. The move itself performs no audio rendering. Normal playback/export performs the stretch in small cached blocks. A seek restarts the stretch with a short preroll; its waveform phase can differ from uninterrupted playback, while both follow the same beat clock. The tempo points stay where you set them when moving a clip.

**Waveform: energy** is the default. Its single-sided contour emphasizes rises, breakdowns and quiet passages without a full-height peak outline obscuring them. Close zoom adds transient detail. **Waveform: detail** always shows the RMS body and peak outline; **Waveform: peaks** shows amplitude only. The overview uses cached energy at a fixed display scale; these views never change audio gain or timing. Stronger guides and alternating bands count **four bars / 16 beats in 4/4**, using the same integrated tempo map as snapping. Individual bars and beats appear as space permits. This is a counting grid, not automatic musical-phrase detection. Beat numbers **1–4** identify known downbeats; unknown grids stay explicit.

Orange **IN / OUT** handles initially meet at the default overlap boundary and align consecutive tracks. Drag OUT to move the incoming track and all following tracks so its IN meets that OUT. Drag IN to choose a different source position while sliding its track under the previous OUT; later tracks follow. The first unpaired IN and final OUT can move freely. Pairing uses timeline order, including projects saved with unaligned markers. Fades remain separate and the full source stays available. Track effects and source-owned automation travel with each moved track; tempo points and recording mode stay unchanged. Moving through an existing tempo change uses the same immediate elastic playback as moving a clip. Snap uses whole source beats; turn Snap off or hold Alt for free movement. Each drag has one Undo step. A linked move before session zero is rejected intact.

Right-click a clip for **Cut / Copy / Paste / Delete**, volume and three-band EQ markers, labels, VST parameter markers, loop markers, split, reverse, track settings and BPM/key/downbeat tweaks. Paste targets the clicked lane/time. **Ctrl+X/C/V**, **V/T/M/B/E**, **9/0**, **S**, **Ctrl+W** and **Ctrl+T** expose the same commands at the cursor. Effects markers require an automatable VST parameter. Loop Start followed by Loop End replaces the clip with two repetitions of that source range; Undo restores it, and Escape cancels an unfinished loop start. Splitting preserves an existing fade and keeps the pieces adjacent in the playlist. Copy/paste operates on whole clips. A changing-tempo clip requires Beatmatch before pasting into a legacy fixed-tempo session.

Automatic overlapping transitions align known first downbeats to four-beat bars and share one BPM ramp. Downbeat inference combines recurring bass accents with repeated phrase changes across the whole track, then finds the first audible attack on that bar phase. Quiet opening percussion is retained before the later full drums. A single loud pickup cannot declare beat one. Equal kicks without structural cues, conflicting evidence and variable tempo can leave **beat 1 ?**; those tracks still use their detected pulse grid. This is a conservative **4/4 inference**, informed by REA observations of MixMeister; general meter detection and complete algorithm parity are not claimed. A loop that does not span whole four-beat bars does not claim bar identity. Cut retains beat-level placement.

To correct a grid, zoom in and right-click the waveform at the first downbeat, then choose **Set first downbeat here (4/4)**. Or enter exact source seconds in **BPM / Key → First downbeat**. The correction applies to that source's clips, is saved with the session and in the application analysis store when writable, and treats the existing first-beat override as bar one. Use **Beatmatch** to rebuild transitions after correcting it; that command rearranges the mix. Manual dragging still snaps one beat at a time. Existing sessions keep their placement on reopening. Old automatic analysis refreshes once to add downbeat facts while retaining manual corrections. Ambiguous tracks should be checked by eye and ear.

Tempo edits prepare pitch-preserving audio in the background, briefly pausing and then resuming playback. Synced clips retain their source content and musical placement as their duration changes. Splits, reverse and loops remain available. Undo/Redo restores both tempo markers and prepared audio. Save/Export waits for pending tempo edits. Audio-track volume, pan, EQ and VST automation use source positions and follow moves, lane changes and tempo edits. Master automation remains global. Unaffected prepared audio is reused. Completed preparations use disk mappings, including in undo history, so their combined size can exceed 1 GiB. The 1 GiB preparation limit applies to actual allocated PCM buffers and each single output.

Sessions from before version 6 keep their original fixed tempo until you use Beatmatch or edit the tempo map. Versions 6–11 retain their existing tempo maps, including additional points from earlier automatic transitions. **Beatmatch** rebuilds the one-target-per-track defaults and rearranges the mix; reopening alone preserves your saved edits.

## Label markers and tracklists

Press **L** or use **Mix → Label marker at playhead** to name a section. Double-click the **LABELS** strip between the time ruler and tempo lane to create a label there, or double-click an existing label to edit its name and exact session seconds. Click a label to select it and seek. Drag horizontally to move it with beat snapping; hold **Alt** for free placement. Labels can cross one another but cannot occupy exactly the same time. Right-click a label, or select it and press **Delete**, to remove it. **[** and **]** jump to the previous/next label; the Mix menu offers the same commands. Label edits support Undo/Redo.

Labels stay at absolute session times when clips or tempo change. They do not alter beat detection, infer a downbeat, split audio, or extend the WAV export beyond the last clip. Sessions from versions 1–6 open with no labels.

**File → Import M3U into library** reads local UTF-8 `.m3u`/`.m3u8` playlists. You can also drop these files onto the app or pass an absolute playlist path at startup. Relative source paths resolve against the playlist's folder; spaces, Unicode, hash and percent characters in filenames are preserved. The library retains one entry per source file; playlist order is retained internally, while the library's chosen sort still controls its display. Import does not append or replace timeline clips. Double-click imported tracks to arrange them. Playlist paths are checked in the background before importing; Stop cancels this stage without changing the library or mix. Existing audio-import cancellation applies once decoding begins. M3U metadata does not replace detected/manual BPM or key. URLs, HLS streams, nested playlists, drive-relative paths and non-UTF-8 text are rejected with a reason; playlists have an 8 MiB input text budget and a 64 MiB resolved-path budget including object/allocation overhead.

**File → Export source playlist as M3U** writes UTF-8 source paths in chronological clip order, including repeats. Entries refer to the complete original files, with their source durations. Trims, loops, reverse, overlaps, mix tempo, effects and automation are retained only by `.mixworks`; use **Export WAV** to hear the finished mix in another player. **Export mix tracklist as text** writes clip start/length, lane, source title, source BPM, note/Camelot key, source path and label times. Both exports back up an existing destination and preserve previous backups.

## Volume and EQ automation

Right-click a waveform to add automation markers. Drag a node to change time/value, right-click a node to delete it, or double-click for precise entry. Beat snapping applies to mouse edits; hold Alt to bypass it. Click the ruler to seek. Selecting or moving nodes never reveals an automation toolbar or changes the timeline layout. Master curves display on the first lane.

Select a node, then use **Mix / Automation** (also available in the timeline right-click menu). **Read selected curve** toggles playback of its saved points; off retains the points and uses the manual value. **Point at playhead...** opens precise point entry. **Clear selected curve** removes only that parameter's points. Read and Clear support Undo/Redo and respect transition locks. **Choose VST parameter...** selects an automatable parameter on the selected track. `[A]` in the lane header indicates active automation.

Audio-track points use source-elapsed seconds and are displayed at their current timeline positions; master and unused mixer-lane points use session seconds. Endpoints are held within the owning audio track. Volume/EQ/master interpolate in dB (-60 to +12); pan displays -100% left to +100% right; VST values display 0-100% of the plugin's normalized range. The plugin defines discrete steps and its physical units. Audio-track points follow moves, lane changes and tempo edits; splitting retains the corresponding portion of each curve. Clip fades/gain and mute/solo still apply. Volume, pan and master are evaluated per sample; EQ and VST playback use a fixed 32-sample session clock. Live playback and WAV export use the same renderer. VST targets retain their instance identity and stable parameter ID through saving and insert removal; unavailable targets fail reopening without replacing the current session.

Use the live desk's **Record**, **Play** and **Stop** controls to capture a performance. Normal mouse/touch control needs no arming or MIDI mapping. The optional **Mix / Automation / Recording options** submenu retains Off, Touch, Latch and individual lane/master arming for advanced use. Existing MIDI mappings remain supported; see [live desk operation](../LIVE-DESK.md).

**Read / safe** plays enabled curves without recording. **Touch** writes while controls move, holds for 500 ms after the last message, then returns to the prior curve over 50 ms; FLkey does not send physical-touch messages. **Write / latch** starts on the first movement and keeps writing held values until pause, stop, seek, disarm or a mode change. Each parameter writes only its touched region, preserving enabled automation outside it, with a 50 ms return at punch-out. Starting from a disabled curve replaces its dormant points with a new take based on the manual value. Stop returns to Read; sessions always reopen disarmed in Read. Timeline edits and parameter reassignment end recording. Undo restores the recording pass, including manual VST values. Recording timestamps are captured from the audio playhead on MIDI arrival, with device-buffer timing resolution; writing happens on the UI thread.

Version 10 sessions retain source alignment markers and separate fade positions for split clips; versions 1–9 remain readable. Older IN/OUT values become alignment-only markers. Label markers, tempo maps, curves, VST identities and Read flags remain supported. Native VST-editor gestures are not recorded; use mapped FLkey controls or edit the curve. Audio/MIDI note recording remains future work; clip-relative automation is supported in the current format.

Library titles hide short leading track-number prefixes for display (for example `01. Blade Runnerz` becomes `Blade Runnerz`); original names, files and media IDs stay intact. **TITLE** sorts A–Z by the displayed title by default; click again for Z–A. Number-led names such as `2 One 2` and four-digit years stay visible when they do not look like a track index.

## FLkey 49

Open **Audio / MIDI** and enable the FLkey's **MIDI** input. Mixworks automatically enables an available FLkey input whose name does not contain `DAW` at startup. Keys and velocity, pitch bend, modulation and other unassigned MIDI messages go to the selected track's VST3 instrument. The on-screen keyboard works as an alternative. Inputs can also be enabled manually for other controllers.

Use **Audio / MIDI → FLkey guided setup** with the FLkey's controls in **Custom Mode**. Move only the control named on screen. Existing assignments are kept; repeated messages from one control cannot fill later steps. The guided layout is:

| Control | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Faders 1–4 | `volume-1` through `volume-4` |
| Fader 9 | `master` |
| Knobs 1–4 | `pan-1` through `pan-4` |
| Pads/buttons 1–4 | `select-1` through `select-4` |
| Pads/buttons 5–8 | `mute-1` through `mute-4` |
| Pad 14 | Return to start |
| Pads 15–16 | Play/pause, stop |
| Knobs 5–8 | Selected track's plugin controls 1–4 |

For plugin steps, turn the physical knob when prompted. After setup, select the desired mixer track and VST3 insert, click **MIDI controls**, choose Control 1–4 and a named plugin parameter, then **Assign**. Save the `.mixworks` session to retain these choices. For example, Control 1 can target cutoff and Control 2 resonance. Assignments use the plugin's stable parameter ID rather than its position in a list. The dialog lists all eight available plugin controls; individual MIDI Learn can map additional controls. Defaults are drawn from the plugin's actual exposed automatable parameters and can be changed.

Mappings use the received CC/note number and channel, rather than guessed factory values, and save to `%APPDATA%/Mixworks/midi-mappings.json` with a backup. Relearning an action replaces its previous binding. A mapped note/pad is reserved for the action. Standard MIDI Start/Continue/Stop messages are also supported. FL Studio-specific DAW handshakes, display/LED feedback and automatic factory-mode mappings are not implemented. The `MIDIIN2` port is optional and is not automatically enabled. Novation documents the MIDI/DAW port distinction and configurable messages in [its FLkey guide](https://userguides.novationmusic.com/hc/en-gb/articles/23741653074066-Working-with-the-FLkey-49-in-FL-Studio).

## Saved track analysis

Track analysis is stored in `%LOCALAPPDATA%/Mixworks/Analysis-v1`, using a hash of the absolute source path as its filename. Each record contains the source path, audio fingerprint, detected BPM, beat/downbeat origins, confidence, grid deviation, key and alternative key, tuning, examined windows, compact waveforms and manual corrections. Audio bytes are not modified, and no new analysis files are written into music folders. Changed records retain one previous-version `.bak` in the application folder; identical saves do nothing. Older detector versions cannot replace newer records.

Subsequent imports and sessions reuse that record when its SHA-256 audio fingerprint, decoded dimensions and detector revision match. Changed audio is analysed again even if its size and date are unchanged. Outdated or malformed automatic analysis is recomputed; independently valid manual corrections remain when the source identity still matches. A saved session's explicit corrections take precedence over defaults from the track record. Legacy `Song.mp3.mixworks-analysis.json` files remain readable and are migrated after verification; normal loading leaves the legacy file untouched. Moving audio to a new path requires analysis again unless a valid legacy record accompanies it.

For an explicit migration without decoding, `Mixworks.exe --migrate-analysis "<absolute-folder>" --report "<new-absolute-report.json>"` verifies and copies supported records from that folder only. It preserves originals and existing central records. Inspect each report row's `error`: stale fingerprints or unsupported records are left for normal loading to refresh. The command's exit status confirms report creation, not that every record migrated.

The import/open status reports how many saved analyses were reused. **BPM / Key** shows the record path and any save problem. Read-only folders and conflicting/unidentified files do not prevent playback: analysis runs in memory and a warning explains why it could not be saved. Unknown BPM/key results remain unknown. Files with the same name but different audio extensions have distinct records.

The read-only `--analyse` command can reuse records and decoded caches but does not create/update analysis records or persist new decoded PCM.

## Remembered library and cover art

Mixworks saves the library in `%LOCALAPPDATA%/Mixworks/library.json`, including track identities, metadata, BPM/key/beat analysis, manual corrections, compact peak/RMS waveforms and embedded cover thumbnails. Startup restores these display facts together without reopening every source or loading playback PCM. Available snapshots remain browsable even when a drive is disconnected. File size/date and analysis-record changes are checked on an idle worker; full source fingerprints are verified before playback. A fresh drop from a stale catalogue loads the current file and duration. Manual corrections transfer only when the source identity still matches. Background refresh replaces the library entry while existing clips and Undo retain their already-loaded source objects. New mix retains the library; opening another session merges its sources. Restoring alone does not make the mix unsaved.

Library and Explorer drops appear immediately. Library clips use their saved duration and waveform; previously unknown external files show an explicitly pending duration until the worker reads it. Batch files follow one another once durations are known, while deliberate moves are retained. Preparing/unavailable audio is labelled on the clip and in the playlist. You can keep arranging and Undo/Redo while loading; late completion cannot resurrect an undone clip or a new mix. Playback, Save and Export wait for audio readiness, and changed sources cannot silently replace the saved clip's audio/geometry. Explicitly reimport a failed source to update the library, then remove any old unavailable clip. New tempo/stretch preparation can still take time.

The catalogue retains offline paths and merges snapshots additively, with atomic writes, idempotence, preserved unknown top-level fields and backups. Malformed, unidentified or newer catalogues are preserved. Its bounded input/storage budget is 128 MiB; compact waveforms avoid JSON numbers for every sample bucket. Original audio is still required for playback; decoded PCM remains in the separate evictable cache. Old path-only catalogues upgrade once on an idle worker. For a headless upgrade without changing source audio/sidecars or persisting new decoded PCM, run `Mixworks.exe --prepare-library "<absolute-library.json>" --report "<absolute-report.json>"`.

The Library now displays cover thumbnails with title and artist, falling back to the filename and an **MW** tile when tags/artwork are missing. Search also matches artist, album and filename. Hover over a row to see the original audio path, album and artwork source. On Windows, the worker first asks the local audio thumbnail/property handlers for a cover, title, artist and album. Availability depends on the file and installed Windows handlers; an actual MP3 with embedded ID3 artwork/tags was verified. If no audio thumbnail is available, Mixworks checks the track's filename stem, then `cover`, `folder`, `front`, and `album` beside the audio, trying JPG, JPEG and PNG for each. It does not perform online cover searches. Sources are checked again on loading rather than treating an old image as authoritative.

Each thumbnail is at most 64 pixels on its longest side. Local image input is limited to 16 MiB and 16 megapixels before scaling. Bad/missing artwork falls back without blocking analysis or playback. Both **Library** and **Playlist** have an **ART** thumbnail column. Click its header to group matching artwork, and click again to reverse group order; missing artwork stays last. Grouping compares local thumbnail pixels, so alternate cover images may form separate groups even for one album. Playlist sorting changes only the displayed rows, never timeline placement. Click **START** to display the playlist chronologically again. Panel splitter movement retains column widths and order; resize or drag the headers themselves to change them. Narrow panels scroll horizontally rather than squeezing columns.

## Faster session reopening

Mixworks retains decoded audio and pitch-preserving tempo output under `%LOCALAPPDATA%/Mixworks/AudioCache-v1`. This is separate from preserved analysis records under `%LOCALAPPDATA%/Mixworks/Analysis-v1`. Imports and session opening populate the caches automatically. A cold cache may still require decoding and preparation; later opens can reuse them, including after closing and restarting Mixworks. The opening status reports reused analysis, decoded and tempo-cache counts. Unused session-library entries retain verified source facts, analysis and waveforms without loading playback PCM. Adding one to the timeline loads its audio in the background, usually from the decoded cache.

Source SHA-256 fingerprints and dimensions identify decoded audio. Tempo caches also identify the stretch ratio or the trimmed/reversed/looped source section and the intersecting tempo map. Session saves retain enough numeric precision to preserve fractional-tempo cache identities. Cache headers, dimensions, payload checksums and finite samples are validated before use. Changed source content invalidates its audio caches even if size/date were retained. A changed tempo map, source section, reverse or loop phase selects a new preparation. Missing or damaged owned entries are rebuilt; an unidentified file is preserved. The original audio files remain required.

The combined persistent cache has an **8 GiB disk budget**. New writes evict unused, identified cache entries in access-time order. Active Windows memory mappings remain available and are not deleted. If the cache is full, unwritable, unidentified or busy, playback uses temporary decoded audio/in-memory preparation and reports a cache warning. **BPM / Key** shows the decoded-cache location and source cache warnings. Prepared-cache write warnings are also reported in the opening status. Persistent caches are disposable acceleration data; sessions and source audio remain authoritative.

Reopening still reads source bytes to verify identity, validates needed PCM checksums and finite samples, loads plugin state and builds the session. Disk speed, cache capacity and plugin loading affect the improvement; reopening is not instantaneous. On 19 October 2026, two real recordings loaded individually from warm caches in 0.7–1.5 seconds; their two-track mix reopened through actual Save/Open in 4.7 seconds with all audio caches reused. The production cache was 7.97 GiB of its 8 GiB limit, so it could not retain every library decode and tempo variant. These measurements describe this host and two recordings, not a general latency guarantee; see the loading investigation in HANDOVER.md.

## Automatic Music folder scanning

Mixworks scans Windows' default **Music** folder and its subfolders while idle. Automatic scanning is enabled by default, starts shortly after an idle launch, and checks again about a minute after each completed/stopped pass. Playback, imports, session opening, exports and tempo preparation take priority. The scanner finishes its current file before yielding; it starts no new analysis during playback. It runs only while Mixworks is open.

New supported audio files are analysed and added to the **Library**, with analysis and decoded caches saved in the application data folder where possible. Scanned entries keep source facts and waveforms without pinning decoded PCM or retaining temporary playback files, so a large scan can recycle the bounded disk cache. Existing library files with missing/stale analysis records are refreshed without duplicates. Source identity determines whether manual corrections can be retained. Refreshing a changed file supplies a new library entry for future drops while existing timeline clips keep their loaded source and tempo. Source and analysis-record size/date changes trigger checks between passes; full content fingerprints are verified when a source is loaded. Failed/unsavable unchanged candidates are not retried every pass during the current run; a file/record change, reopening or an explicit scan retries them.

Use **Tools → Scan Music folder now**, **Automatically scan Music folder**, or **Stop current Music scan**. Scan now performs one pass even when automatic scanning is disabled. Stop cancels the current pass; disable automatic scanning to keep it off. The enabled preference persists in `%LOCALAPPDATA%/Mixworks/library-scan.json`, with backups and unknown fields preserved. Music-folder scanning does not create or change arrangements automatically.

## Beatmatching and musical key

Detection development currently prioritizes **constant-tempo 4/4**. A reliable downbeat anchor anywhere in a track establishes bar phase; the grid extends backwards and forwards from it. Finding the earliest downbeat is optional for arrangement. Beat alignment remains available when beat one is unknown. With each verified detector update, retained development analysis/corrections, audio caches, library snapshots and recovered mixes are cleared; ordinary launches reuse current analysis. Use a fresh mix when evaluating an updated detector.

Imports combine local Beat This! pulse/meter predictions with percussive timing, periodicity and recurring structural evidence. Key estimation uses tuning-corrected chroma, register normalization, stereo harmonic views and major/minor profiles, with noise and overtone rejection. The library selector offers **Note**, **Camelot**, or **Note + Camelot**; BPM and key columns sort and search, and compatible keys are highlighted relative to the selected clip. **BPM / Key** shows confidence, alternative key, tuning, grid deviation and examined duration, and accepts manual BPM, first-beat and key corrections. Its **Pulse correction** menu offers **Half BPM** and **Double BPM**, applied to the entered BPM or detection. Manual BPM editing retains four decimal places. `?` marks low confidence; `~` marks inconsistent tempo/grid estimates. Confidence values are evidence scores, not calibrated accuracy percentages.

Distributed physical timing windows and source attack fits calibrate the neural interpretation and reduce long-track drift. Contiguous 20-second physical checks then verify local consistency, including passages with sparse neural predictions; physical fits supply independent tempo/phase proposals. Corroborated conflicts require grid review and suppress a confident fixed-grid downbeat. Competing metrical interpretations are considered beyond the old single-winner/median design. Grid deviation describes fit residuals, not musical accuracy. Unknown downbeats remain explicit and can be corrected on the waveform; semantic verse/chorus labeling is not implemented, and bar lengths assume 4/4. Changing MIX BPM prepares synced clips off the audio thread, with pitch preserved by [SoundTouch](https://www.surina.net/soundtouch/). Live playback and WAV export use the same prepared PCM. Manual source-BPM correction rebuilds preparation even when the mix map is unchanged. An automatic refresh retains captured arrangement clocks; use **Beatmatch** explicitly to apply a changed automatic BPM and rebuild placement.

Analysis revision 9 invalidates older automatic results while retaining valid manual corrections. Missing/corrupt model or runtime failures remain explicit and cannot become successful current cache entries. Bass/accent and structural evidence retain quiet opening downbeats where supported; disagreement can leave bar one unknown. Prepared audio has a separate revision. Saved arrangements retain their captured source clocks and transition identities. See the [current outcome assessment](../RHYTHM-TIMING-REVIEW.md), [earlier measured detection and alignment results](../research/DETECTION-V032.md), the [v0.31 baseline](../research/DETECTION-AND-ALIGNMENT.md) and [earlier rhythm findings](../research/RHYTHM-RELIABILITY.md).

The synthetic checks cover all 24 major/minor keys, detuning with drums/noise, rejection of noise and harmonic-rich single notes, several tempos, long-track drift, silent sections, conflicting grids and retained pitch. A fresh 77-recording comparison agrees with DJ.Studio within 0.1 BPM on 77 tracks, with its keys on 43 and stored Mixed in Key values on 48. Downbeat remains unknown on 11; 22 available bar phases disagree. Eight separate holdout tempos agree with native DJ AI, but five raw bar phases disagree. Reference labels are comparisons, not adjudicated truth; commercial detection parity is unproven. See the measured report for executable provenance. The chroma approach follows published [key-detection methods](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2006/paper/000134.pdf); spectral-peak/tuning choices were checked against the [HPCP reference](https://essentia.upf.edu/reference/std_HPCP.html). No Essentia dependency was added.

## Current boundaries

- `.mmp` compatibility and exact Fusion DSP remain to be reconstructed. General MixMeister-level downbeat accuracy, automatic phrase selection for transitions, correction of changing tempo inside an individual source recording, and commercial key-detector parity remain unverified. The editable mix tempo map assumes a stable or manually corrected source BPM.
- Four stereo lanes. Decoded and prepared PCM use read-only disk mappings; completed cold preparations release their RAM buffers too. If a persistent preparation cannot be saved, an owned temporary mapping is attempted. Temporary decoded/prepared files under `%TEMP%/Mixworks-PCM` are deleted when their last reference is released. Allow disk space for uncompressed audio. One file's decode/cache entry and one preparation output are each limited to 1 GiB. Actual allocated preparation PCM, including retained RAM fallbacks and the next cold output, shares a 1 GiB budget; mapped playback and undo audio do not consume that allocation budget. Multichannel imports use their first two channels. The OS may keep mapped pages in physical memory and incur disk reads; dedicated playback prefetch remains future work.
- VST3 runs inside the host process. Stereo main buses only; no VST2/32-bit bridge, sidechain routing, plugin crash isolation or plugin delay compensation yet. A faulty plugin can terminate the host. Multi-class VST3 modules are rejected explicitly.
- VST instruments can be played live. MIDI recording, piano-roll sequencing, audio recording and instrument-only offline MIDI renders are not implemented yet.
- Fade envelopes, clip gain, three-band EQ, volume/EQ/pan/master/VST curves and armed FLkey/host-control automation recording are available. Native VST-editor gesture recording, other built-in effects and effect-tail export remain future work.
- Session library audio reuses saved analysis, decoded and tempo caches when valid. Streaming decoders and faster cold starts remain future work. Older version 1/2 saves contain only sources used by clips; unused imports from those builds require reimport into the current build before they can be saved.

### Timeline height, eight lanes and Windows audio (0.53)

Choose **Lanes: 1–8** above the lane headers. Four remain the default; choosing more exposes new empty lanes. Reducing the visible count keeps any occupied higher lanes reachable by vertical scrolling. All eight support audio, inserts, automation, mute/solo, pan, LPF and MIDI mapping. Existing four-lane sessions open unchanged, with neutral extra lanes; new saves use format 24. The live desk's paired pots can select any of the eight lanes, excluding their partner. Its existing level/pan faders and lower pads still address lanes 1–4; lanes 5–8 have their own lane-header controls and named MIDI actions.

When **Controller and Transitions are both undocked**, drag the grip beneath the tempo footer to resize the timeline. It initially fills the available height. Dock either panel to restore its normal lower workspace.

The toolbar BPM follows the arrangement tempo at the playhead during playback, including ramps, even when another marker is selected. When stopped, selecting a marker still exposes its editable BPM. Typing/dragging is protected from timer refresh; editing during playback targets the playhead.
