# Visual expansion proposal — 3 November 2026

Status: larger expansion remains proposed. Jay approved five image-generator
examples plus brighter palettes and continuous particles; that
[preview checkpoint](VISUAL-FIVE-PREVIEW-20261103.md) is implemented and verified
within its stated scope. Its five additions count towards the eighty below;
seventy-five remain pending preview feedback.
Existing catalogue: 123 scenes. Proposed addition: ten families of eight scenes,
giving 203 scenes. Palette choices and named looks do not count towards that total.

## Requested outcome

Jay wants stronger palette variety, including bright neon; far less repetition of
wavy surface veins across a mix; particles that continue through image resolution;
and around 80 new scenes that depart from the existing formulas. Preserve the
current catalogue and the running production session.

Source inspection found 56 active scenes in fourteen groups using one layered
relief renderer, and 28 sprite-particle scenes. Shared surface code includes
repeated contour-line treatments; the relief backdrop also shares a nested sine
pattern. These are plausible contributors to the perceived repetition, not a
complete visual diagnosis. Automatic arrangement balances scene keys and broad
energy styles, but does not currently penalise repeated material motifs. Both the
particle simulation and fragment vertex template deliberately hold the image at
beats 12–14 of each sixteen-beat cycle.

## Recommended approach

### Subsequent reference direction from Jay

Jay particularly likes image-using Synesthesia scenes: Terrain Lattice, Swarm,
Sprinkles, Shrouded, Redrum, Quicksand, Overload, Nova, Myoplasm, Molten,
Lost in the Sauce, Lattix, Hue Review, Hexoplanet, Glob, Glitch, Glassier,
Gaussian Smoothlife, Bouncy Hexagons and Biopsy V2. He asks whether Alloy can
match them. Treat these twenty as named creative references for the ongoing
expansion, not approval of every concept below or a request to replace the
existing catalogue.

This sharpens the priority towards images participating in generation: source
luminance, edges and colour can seed evolving state, steer motion, modulate
geometry, or supply spatial materials. Readability can emerge and dissolve;
continuous visibility of an intact photograph is not the sole success criterion.
Maintain the earlier requirement for frequent readable complete images across
the overall mix, and continuous motion through particle assembly.

The [installed-scene mechanism index](commercial/SYNESTHESIA-SCENE-INDEX-20261102.md)
and [earlier technique audit](commercial/SYNESTHESIA-TECHNIQUE-AUDIT-20261102.md)
provide relevant private research. Their mechanism descriptions inform feasibility;
their dated gap assessments are not a current runtime audit. Existing Alloy study
names and historical approximate recipes do not prove parity. Some references
were associated with recipes now retired; do not automatically restore those
entries or override retained choices.

Rebalance the proposed eighty towards genuinely different image-driven systems
before expanding all ten initial families. Prioritise representative cellular
growth, interacting particles, evolving liquid/feedback fields, spatial image
geometry and selective signal processing. Mechanical, type and shadow concepts
remain candidates for contrast rather than displacing this newly clarified taste.
The final distribution of eighty is still proposed.

For each chosen reference, compare an original Alloy implementation using the
same owned input image and soundtrack at multiple moments. Evaluate characteristic
motion, image causality, colour/depth and behaviour between attacks. Native runtime
delivery and resource use need separate proof. Aim for comparable visual richness
and musical behaviour; do not promise exact matching of all twenty from names or
source inspection. No new reference render or scene implementation was performed
for this follow-up.

Use shared palette/material controls where appropriate, but develop ten genuinely
different visual instruments. Prove one representative scene from each family
before expanding to its eight compositions. A scene must differ in its spatial
structure, motion and musical behaviour; new names, colours, seeds, rotations or
reflection counts alone do not qualify.

Recolouring and adding presets alone would address colour cheaply but leave the
same visual grammar. Generating eighty more relief recipes would raise the count
quickly but repeat the current problem. Ten distinct instruments require more
authoring and visual review, but best match the requested experience. Shared
rendering utilities are useful; a single universal scene formula is not the goal.

The first observable milestone is a short, real soundtrack preview of ten family
representatives, saturated palette choices, varied surfaces and continuous
particle resolve. Success means the families are recognisable from their movement
and silhouettes even with colour removed. This is a suggested implementation
sequence, not an additional restriction on Jay's authorised development scope.

## Colour and surface changes

Offer palette selection independently of scene selection, with scene-authored
defaults and selectable saturation, contrast and glow. Proposed palette sets:

- Electric: cyan, hot magenta and ultraviolet on near-black.
- Acid: fluorescent lime, violet and electric blue.
- Laser primaries: saturated red, green and blue with restrained white highlights.
- Solar: vivid scarlet, orange and yellow against deep indigo.
- Tropical: turquoise, fuchsia and bright tangerine.
- Jewel: saturated emerald, ruby and sapphire.
- Graphic: black/white with one selectable vivid accent.
- Soft: retain subdued choices for quieter passages and personal preference.

Retain strong dark areas and bounded highlights so neon does not wash to white.
Apply colours to actual forms and optional image recolouring, with an image-colour
preservation choice. Phrase changes can move between palettes; musical attacks
can shift accents. Avoid turning every scene into the same continuous rainbow.

Broaden surface vocabulary: clean unpatterned colour, brushed metal, stipple,
halftone, checker/crosshatch, faceted enamel, etched straight circuits, speckled
ceramic and glass, alongside occasional veins. Patterns must attach to the form
and suit its material. New families should have their own materials by default.

Automatic selection should consider family, dominant motif, material pattern,
composition and motion as separate traits. Target no adjacent family repeats and
an eight-scene cooldown for a distinctive pattern such as veins, where the
eligible pool permits. Prefer unused scenes across the whole mix; changing the
colour of an already-used pattern does not reset its cooldown. In a small selected
pool, relax the oldest cooldown deterministically instead of failing or leaving
a gap. Manual choices remain available. Preserve the existing occasional-particle
policy, and avoid forcing every new family to show images or kaleidoscopes.

## Continuous particle resolution

Make image assembly a point on a continuous trajectory: converge, briefly read
as the complete image, then carry through into the next formation. Remove the
flat recall plateau in both the simulation and fragment drawing, not just one.
Do not suppress velocity with a high-drag home lock or disguise the hold by
moving the camera. Coordinate fragment orientation, source sampling and coverage
so the image remains legible around the crossing without snapping into place.

Use family-specific departures: pass through the image plane, peel into an
opposite curl, continue through a travelling weave, or overshoot into an orbit.
Maintain momentum at the resolve and smoothly vary the pace around it. Keep
normal transport pause behaviour; continuity is required while playback advances.
Check muted/quiet passages too so a lack of attacks does not recreate a freeze.

## Ten proposed families — eighty compositions

These are authored concepts, not claims of completed scenes or independent
simulation engines. Each family has a distinct primary mechanism. Feasibility
and visual difference must be demonstrated in the native renderer.

### 1. Volumetric light choreography

Light occupies dark space; visible depth and occlusion replace patterned surfaces.
Bass shapes beam breadth and reach, mids steer fixtures, treble articulates edges.

1. **Light Guillotines:** thin luminous planes shear through intersecting volumes.
2. **Laser Harp:** taut light strings pluck and settle at different depths.
3. **Crossfire:** opposing beam banks sweep and cross without a central tunnel.
4. **Fan Vault:** a ceiling of light fans opens in staggered phrases.
5. **Prism Relay:** separated optical blocks hand a split beam across the stage.
6. **Light Elevator:** luminous slabs climb past one another in a deep shaft.
7. **Beacon Field:** widely spaced cones scan a dark floor with offset timing.
8. **Auroral Columns:** upright volumes compress and exchange height in waves.

### 2. Impact machines

Visible contact, leverage and momentum; solid moving mechanisms rather than
decorative relief gears. Bass launches impacts, mids advance mechanisms, treble
triggers small linked reactions.

1. **Domino Cascade:** collisions propagate along rebuilding paths.
2. **Piston Choir:** opposing piston banks compress a central solid form.
3. **Pendulum Exchange:** swinging masses pass momentum across a row.
4. **Ratchet Lift:** pawls drive rising teeth with visible catch and release.
5. **Ball Run:** spheres traverse ramps, gates and switches.
6. **Cam Ballet:** differently shaped cams lift followers in interlocking rhythms.
7. **Stamping Press:** punches emboss and release a moving sheet.
8. **Counterweight Stack:** suspended blocks trade height through visible linkages.

### 3. Elastic sculpture

Continuous deformable bodies with recoverable strain, not particle image fragments.
Bass applies load, mids twist constraints, treble excites smaller ripples.

1. **Rubber Citadel:** inflated towers squash, lean and recover.
2. **Tension Net:** anchors pull a suspended mesh into changing saddles.
3. **Membrane Drum:** local strikes propagate over a stretched translucent skin.
4. **Gel Stack:** soft blocks compress and slide against one another.
5. **Spring Orchard:** weighted stems bend with delayed follow-through.
6. **Balloon Crush:** packed bladders displace each other inside a frame.
7. **Elastic Knot:** a thick loop tightens, stretches and rolls through itself.
8. **Hanging Sail:** a single broad sheet billows between moving attachments.

### 4. Liquid experiments

Bounded liquid behaviour with distinct boundaries and forcing; avoid substituting
animated noise or contour bands for the stated effect. Bass injects pressure,
mids move boundaries, treble excites fine surface motion.

1. **Wave Tank:** paddles create crossing waves against visible tank walls.
2. **Ferro Crown:** a magnetic field raises and lowers glossy liquid spikes.
3. **Bubble Raft:** packed bubbles merge, divide and rearrange under compression.
4. **Ink Collision:** two colour fronts collide, curl and separate.
5. **Capillary Maze:** liquid advances through branching narrow channels.
6. **Viscous Pour:** a thick stream buckles and coils on a receiving surface.
7. **Soap Lens:** thin films stretch between moving frames with interference colour.
8. **Droplet Impact:** falling drops create crowns and interacting expanding rings.

### 5. Analogue video instruments

Raster, signal timing and bounded frame feedback produce structured electronic
motion. No universal noise-marbling overlay. Bass drives sweep displacement, mids
phase relationships, treble fine signal articulation.

1. **Vector Phosphor:** traced vector figures leave controlled decaying trails.
2. **Raster Fold:** scan lines roll through a bending image plane.
3. **Sync Slip:** offset image bands drift, catch and relock.
4. **Chroma Delay:** colour channels arrive with different temporal offsets.
5. **Feedback Stair:** successive bounded frame copies step into a deep diagonal.
6. **Line Memory:** selected scan rows retain different moments of a moving image.
7. **Signal Cut-Up:** rhythmic gates exchange regions between live signal paths.
8. **Phosphor Rain:** columns refresh at staggered phases and decay independently.

### 6. Moving print studio

Bold flat graphic compositions, hard edges and negative space; no embossed
lighting or compulsory glow. Bass changes layout, mids shift registration,
treble articulates marks and boundaries.

1. **Screenprint Slip:** large colour plates slide between aligned and offset states.
2. **Halftone Pressure:** dot fields vary their coverage and spacing under moving loads.
3. **Cut-Paper Chase:** bold silhouettes pursue and mask one another.
4. **Barcode Ballet:** weighted bars exchange widths and slide into interlocking groups.
5. **Poster Tear:** broad torn shapes peel to expose successive graphic layers.
6. **Registration Orbit:** differently coloured print plates orbit a shared alignment.
7. **Negative Space:** expanding cutouts change which of two shapes reads as foreground.
8. **Stencil Conveyor:** moving masks stamp and remove repeating graphic marks.

### 7. Kinetic type and glyphs

Characters are moving structures, with scene-local text or abstract glyphs as the
default; do not impose artist branding. Bass shapes weight/spacing, mids change
arrangement, treble articulates strokes.

1. **Type Foundry:** individual strokes cast, extrude and assemble into characters.
2. **Elastic Alphabet:** glyph bodies stretch between moving baselines.
3. **Word Avalanche:** solid characters tumble and stack under controlled gravity.
4. **Glyph Magnet:** strokes detach and regroup around competing character centres.
5. **Counterform:** camera movement reveals sculpture through the holes in letterforms.
6. **Type Conveyor:** linked character blocks circulate along intersecting tracks.
7. **Stroke Relay:** drawing fronts transfer between connected glyph paths.
8. **Letterpress Depth:** raised characters advance through a deep field and imprint a plane.

### 8. Shadow theatre

The subject is the moving shadow, including transformations that differ from the
visible object. Bass moves light distance, mids articulate objects, treble adds
small independent occluders.

1. **Giant Small:** tiny moving objects cast monumental, changing silhouettes.
2. **Double Sun:** two coloured lights produce shadows that split and recombine.
3. **Impossible Silhouette:** disconnected solids align into a recognisable shadow.
4. **Perforated Moon:** rotating pierced discs project moving spots and crescents.
5. **Shadow Clockwork:** linked occluders cast a mechanism across differently angled walls.
6. **Blind Sweep:** slatted blockers send widening light/shadow bars over a simple set.
7. **Caustic Puppets:** transparent objects move concentrated light patches across a floor.
8. **Depth Screen:** silhouettes travel through translucent screens at different distances.

### 9. Time sculpture

Organised samples of different moments form the scene; this is more than a single
image texture distorted by the current frame. Use bounded history and procedural
moving content when no suitable animated media exists. Bass controls separation,
mids the sampling path, treble local updates.

1. **Slit-Scan River:** a moving sampling slit lays successive moments along a flowing strip.
2. **Echo Statues:** discrete temporal silhouettes occupy a spatial arc.
3. **Time Accordion:** image strips expose progressively older moments as they expand.
4. **Chrono Cubes:** cube faces carry different ages and update in travelling waves.
5. **Motion Loom:** horizontal and vertical time samples cross into a moving tapestry.
6. **Freeze Wake:** a moving object leaves suspended poses that later rejoin it.
7. **History Carousel:** chronological image planes orbit, turn edge-on and exchange order.
8. **Time Excavation:** moving apertures reveal older layers beneath the current picture.

### 10. Spatial paradoxes

Camera-dependent alignment, ambiguous depth and occlusion create the motion;
avoid another patterned tunnel or mirrored relief. Bass opens depth, mids move
viewpoint, treble triggers local realignments.

1. **Anamorphic Assembly:** scattered solids align into a graphic only from passing viewpoints.
2. **Impossible Stair:** disconnected steps form a continuous climb from the moving camera.
3. **Inside-Out Room:** hinged walls exchange interior and exterior readings.
4. **Occlusion Chess:** simple solids repeatedly exchange apparent front/back order.
5. **Parallax Monument:** separated flat slices become a dimensional object as the view moves.
6. **Forced Perspective:** differently sized forms appear equal, then reveal their true scale.
7. **Window Exchange:** moving openings connect views with distinct perspective relationships.
8. **Reversible Solids:** ambiguous wire/solid forms pass through controlled depth reversals.

## Implementation boundaries and acceptance

Keep existing runtime staging and original provenance/licensing practices. New
families may share bounded maths, geometry and sampling utilities while retaining
their own dynamics and composition. Heavy mechanisms such as fluid interaction,
elastic contact and temporal history require feasibility proofs and declared
resource bounds; the descriptions are not evidence that these already run well.
Author an honest stylisation where appropriate, and replace a weak concept rather
than count an effect whose defining behaviour is absent.

First fix the cross-catalogue palette/pattern/resolve foundations and author the
ten representatives. Review short moving previews, then expand coherent batches
to eighty scenes. Each additional composition must have an authored signature
and a meaningful musical action; reject near-duplicates rather than pad the count.
Do not force universal six-look presets onto families where they erase identity.

During implementation use one below-normal build worker and serial focused
checks at useful checkpoints. Check executable controls, assembly crossings,
quiet/loud input, pattern selection and limited-pool fallback, pause/seek,
save/reopen and live scene blends. Inspect actual moving native output and
side-by-side family samples. No build or test is needed for this proposal itself.
Full regression, stress and sustained performance claims remain outside this
design-only task. Artistic acceptance remains Jay's.

Method research included the [skills.sh Shader Craft listing](https://www.skills.sh/minimax-ai/skills/shader-dev),
which catalogues multiple GLSL technique classes. It informed the choice to vary
rendering mechanisms; no third-party skill, shader or dependency was installed.
Scene concepts above are project proposals, not copied shader recipes.
