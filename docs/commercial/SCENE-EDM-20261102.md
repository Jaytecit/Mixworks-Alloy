# Dance-music scene library update 09 — 2 November 2026

Jay accepts the direction of Waterglass/Cathedral but wants movement suited to
electronic dance music at 120–160 BPM. Slow one-way travel is insufficient.
He authorised continuing across the remaining scenes in related groups and
installing the results in the main Alloy build.

## Delivered scope

All 100 existing scenes receive documented independent bass/mid/treble motion
destinations. The 93 generated recipes retain 21 shared algorithm families; the
six authored scenes and attributed CC0 Beats in 4D retain their separate shaders.
This is a grouped library refresh, not 100 independently reconstructed engines or
reference parity. No audio controller, native code, session format or detector changes.

| Group | Musical movement and visual changes |
| --- | --- |

| Signal and image optics | Subdivision-driven displacement, bounded image motion, travelling refraction and rotating directional blur. |

Beat/subdivision phases use Alloy's project beat; their strengths come from real
PCM bands and transient envelopes. Accumulated band phases carry movement between
attacks. Silence retains restrained ambient movement. This assumes an appropriate
project beat/grid; it is not a new beat detector. Existing seek/reset semantics remain.

Generated scenes use linear image light and a hue-preserving highlight shoulder.
World-plane images get one aspect conversion, and reflected boundaries avoid
stretched border streaks. Cutout interpolation excludes hidden transparent RGB.
Image-led scenes retain fitted proportions; intentionally tiled, polar, material
and signal-effect scenes still transform their images. Earlier authored scenes
also receive linear image sampling and improved media treatment.

## Verification and limits

Evidence: `.local/scene-edm-09/`, with source archives/hashes, retained failures,
per-check provenance, rendered frames, eight-beat 120/160 BPM WebPs and rollback.
All GPU work was serial, below normal priority and bounded to 320x180 or 640x360.
No native rebuild/test, full regression, stress, capacity or sustained benchmark.
Shaders were compiled through the actual renderer using the bundled Python.

- All 100 scenes compiled, rendered nonblank distinct captures, preserved pause,
  avoided broad white clipping and reacted separately to three PCM bands. The
  combined accepted set contains 557 scene checks, including endpoint-tempo
  captures for family representatives and each authored scene.
- 42 geometry checks across 14 scenes use raw depth or height/normal output,
  excluding scene lighting, pigment and images. These support the motion claim
  for those scenes; ordinary RGB differences alone do not establish geometry.
- 24 media checks cover actual user artwork, three Image Gallery aspect fixtures
  and hidden-RGB invariance on five cutout paths. Captures inspected visually.
- Six short real-PCM captures use the existing authorised copy of June Montana's
  *I Need Your Love*, seconds 60–66.2. Their project clock is authored at 140 BPM,
  not independently aligned to that track. This is real-PCM rendering evidence,
  not live native playback or listening acceptance. Temporary decoded PCM removed.

Initial failures remain: eight weak band-output cases, then Panorama's treble;
three weak high-band depth responses, then terrain. Corrected scene mappings and
reran affected groups. Geometry comparison now averages an 18-frame sequence so
a reversal zero-crossing cannot conceal the response. The initial media harness
sent unsupported controls to earlier scenes; fixed to honour each descriptor.
Its zero-check report is incomplete and must not be counted as a pass.

Contact sheets and temporal samples were inspected. Related recipes still show
family resemblance, fine geometry can shimmer, and the catalogue has not received
full artistic/listening acceptance from Jay. Custom crates remain deferred.
