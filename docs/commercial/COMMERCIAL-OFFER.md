# Proposed commercial offer and operating requirements

2 November 2026. Planning recommendations only. Nothing here is published as a
customer promise, and the present binary is not cleared for distribution.

## Recommended starting offer

Use a one-time purchase granting indefinite use of the supplied major version
on supported hardware/OS, subject to reviewed terms. Propose one named user,
installation on two owned computers and one concurrent operator. Allow paid DJ
work and commercial output where the customer holds the necessary music/media
rights. Keep projects accessible offline; choose a simple offline-verifiable
licensing design before adding accounts or activation infrastructure.

Make later major upgrades optional purchases. Include a defined ordinary-update
period (12 months is a proposal for costing, not an approved promise). State the
actual supported OS/hardware, supported features and known limitations. Do not
promise lifetime updates, perpetual support, all plug-in compatibility, flawless
automatic detection or live-show performance that has not been demonstrated.

Provide written documentation, one support email route, installation/licensing
help and a defect triage process. Set an achievable response target after
measuring workload. An ordinary support/update window cannot curtail statutory
remedies or applicable mandatory security obligations. A one-time sale therefore
needs an ongoing reserve for fixes and customer issues; it is not support-free.
[UK digital-content guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/consumer-rights-act-2015/consumer-rights-act-2015)
explains the consumer remedy framework.

Do not set a selling price yet. First cost licence/hosting/transaction expenses,
tax treatment, expected support minutes, security maintenance, refunds and legal
clearance. Then test a price with representative users against the complete
workflow. No willingness-to-pay study has been conducted. The current product's
technical promise is not evidence of a sustainable price or support burden.

## Legal owner and first territories

Jay's statement that development is solely Jay plus AI does not select the legal
seller. Sole trader and limited company differ in liability, administration,
taxation and vendor-tier eligibility. A sole trader has personal liability;
a company introduces ongoing filing responsibilities. Obtain a short accountant
and solicitor comparison before entering vendor agreements. If transferring IP
into a company, document the transfer and check each third-party agreement's
assignment rules. No company or tax registration has been made.

The owner review should also confirm that no employment/client contract claims
rights in this work; having no collaborators does not by itself answer that
contract question. No such claim has been identified by this audit.

Recommend considering a UK-first launch while overseas rights, tax and security
obligations are resolved. Do not accidentally offer worldwide checkout before
deciding which countries are supported. A merchant of record may simplify
transaction taxes and checkout, but examine its actual contract, fees, refund
allocation and territorial coverage. A payment processor alone is not necessarily
the seller or tax-remitting party. No provider has been selected or contacted.
[HMRC guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/the-vat-rules-if-you-supply-digital-services-to-private-consumers)
requires attention to customer location and foreign rules for digital services.

## Customer contract brief for a solicitor

The existing personal beta licence does not grant customers the commercial uses
expected from a paid product. Prepare a separate paid licence covering legal
seller identity, permissions/installations, permitted professional use, ownership
of customer projects, optional upgrades, ordinary support/update period,
termination, transfers, third-party licence precedence and lawful warranty/
liability provisions. Keep mandatory rights intact. Do not copy a competitor's
EULA or transplant a blanket beta disclaimer into paid sales.

Checkout must clearly describe the version, price/taxes, compatibility, features,
support, payment obligation and downloadable terms; provide confirmation in a
retainable form. For immediate digital delivery, use the required prior consent
and acknowledgement concerning cancellation rights, and confirm them to the
buyer. Cancellation consent is not a waiver of remedies for faulty content.
See [official online-selling guidance](https://www.gov.uk/online-and-distance-selling-for-businesses/online-selling).
No live checkout or final contract has been created during this audit.

## Data and security

Targeted source inspection found local analysis, loopback scene IPC and explicit
stem setup downloads, not an ordinary music-upload or telemetry path. This is
not a dynamic network audit and does not establish what third-party plug-ins or
future components do. Logs/session files may include paths and track metadata;
design support diagnostics around explicit export and minimisation.

Sales/support introduce customer names, email, transaction references and
possibly activation data. Before collection, document purpose/lawful basis,
retention, processors/transfers, security, deletion/access handling, breach
response, privacy notice, marketing preferences and ICO fee applicability.
Prefer not to collect music or full projects by default. [UK data-protection
guidance](https://www.gov.uk/data-protection-your-business) applies to business
use of personal information even if audio analysis stays on-device.

If selling into the EU, assess Cyber Resilience Act scope for this product and
its connections, plug-ins and update mechanisms. The Commission records
reporting obligations from 11 September 2026 and main obligations from
11 December 2027. Do not claim those future main rules all apply today, or that
offline branding automatically excludes the product. Plan support life,
vulnerability handling, dependency records and security updates with advice.
See the [Commission overview](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cyber-resilience-act).
Also scope applicable accessibility, consumer, privacy and export/sanctions
requirements for the actual sales model and territories; none is certified here.

## Product acceptance before taking payment

The current handover records focused local verification, not a complete release
gate. Reuse existing evidence rather than rerun checks during this paperwork
audit. Before release, qualify the final cleared candidate with the existing
release framework: fresh-machine install/uninstall, notices, offline operation,
save/recovery, correct export, real-track grid correction, audio-device and
plug-in workflows, human listening, clear limitations and novice usability.
Investigate reported desktop responsiveness/resource problems before promising
show reliability. Visual runtime portability and sustained full-resolution
output remain open. Accessibility/readability also need actual user evaluation.

A future explicit release checkpoint should use serial low-disruption builds/
checks under project policy; no stress or full regression was launched for this
audit. Sign the installer/executable with a properly acquired signing identity,
use secure delivery/update verification, publish hashes and establish rollback
and a vulnerability contact. This is a future implementation/qualification scope,
not a claim that these capabilities are present.

Demo music and footage need their own permissions even though none will be
bundled. Prefer original commissioned/cleared examples and retain grants for
recording, editing, synchronisation and marketing use as appropriate. Buying
tracks for listening does not establish all those rights. Avoid endorsement or
compatibility claims based solely on another company's name/logo.
