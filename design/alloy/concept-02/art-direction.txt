Art direction 02 — Precision, with warmth

CENTRAL IDEA
A carefully made audio instrument: cool, precise working surfaces with copper
details that give the software warmth and identity. “Alloy” expresses different
tracks coming together, and the meeting of technical precision with musical
judgment. The timeline and the sound remain the focus of the working interface.

REFERENCE TRANSLATION
1. Rounded silver hexagon: source of the emblem silhouette and signature dial.
   Keep satin metal, the softened corners and a copper signal detail. Simplify
   the engraving for small sizes. Remove molten edges from the everyday UI.
2. Waveform interface: source of the copper/blue signal relationship and tactile
   detail. Preserve Mixworks’ existing workspace arrangement rather than copying
   the generated reference’s layout. A precise playhead replaces the fire seam.
3. Joined waveform ribbon: secondary expressive graphic for splash artwork,
   backgrounds and promotional imagery. It must not compete with the main mark.
4. Metal title: retain confident uppercase lettering and a horizontal copper
   accent. Reduce extrusion, distress and extreme condensation for readability.

IDENTITY SYSTEM
Primary lockup: rounded hexagon containing a simplified continuous audio-wave
stroke, followed by MIXWORKS ALLOY on one baseline. MIXWORKS is semibold;
ALLOY is regular, with slightly more letter spacing. Keep both words legible.
Use a plain single-color version in the working application header.

Prepare three deliberate versions of the same mark:
  • Presentation: satin silver face with restrained copper engraving and depth.
  • Standard: flat silver/white hexagon and clean copper signal stroke.
  • Micro: optically simplified silhouette/stroke at 16, 24 and 32 pixels;
    remove engraving, grain and shadows when they do not survive rasterization.
The identity must work in one color, without lighting or metal effects. Start
with a 24-pixel minimum for the detailed emblem and review actual-size exports.
Clear space: at least one quarter of the emblem’s width around the lockup.
Do not reproduce the reference images’ corner sparkles or generator marks.

MATERIAL HIERARCHY
Working canvas: smooth matte graphite. Waveforms, tables and text receive no
texture. Panel surfaces are separated by value and fine structural rules.
Controls: restrained bevels, shallow inset tracks and small edge highlights.
Brushed metal belongs mainly on rotary caps and the larger brand emblem.
Brand artwork may use richer material depth and a controlled warm rim light.
Avoid scratches, grime, animated reflections and large orange surface fills.

Copper identifies interaction and the first track family. Track identity also
uses lane number/name; selection uses a visible outline and selected state.
Do not recolor a whole waveform when the playhead crosses it. Copper and blue
identify sources, not playback progress or successful synchronization. Keep
uncertain BPM/downbeat analysis visibly uncertain. Do not rely on hue alone.
Verify final text pairs to at least 4.5:1, and meaningful controls/focus to 3:1
against adjacent surfaces. These are acceptance targets, not certified results.

TYPE AND ICONOGRAPHY
Use Segoe UI Variable for the Windows interface, falling back to Segoe UI.
Use a semibold upright sans serif for the wordmark; draw the final lockup as
vectors once its proportions are approved. Avoid metallic gradients in UI text.
Body/table text: proposed 13–14 logical pixels; secondary labels 11–12; transport
readouts 20–24. Numeric values use tabular figures where available. Keep units
visible: dB, Hz/kHz, BPM and seconds. Preserve source names and full tooltips.
Use one consistent 1.5–2-pixel icon stroke; reserve uppercase for short group
labels. Avoid making every command or table cell uppercase.

THE ALLOY DIAL
The hexagon is an excellent grip shape, but its six sides are not the value
scale. Use a rounded hexagonal cap inside a fixed circular scale. A single
radial notch near the cap edge is the pointer; the cap and pointer rotate
together. The surrounding tick marks and label remain stationary. No waveform
emblem on the cap: it adds an ambiguous orientation cue.

Use a 270-degree sweep, from lower left through top to lower right. The body
can rotate smoothly; never snap to its six faces. Show a numeric value beneath
every dial. A copper arc gives secondary feedback; bipolar controls use a
center reference and an arc from center toward the current value. Pan has a
clear center detent; EQ has a 0 dB reference. Taper/range belong to the existing
parameter, not to the decorative geometry. Do not change audio behavior while
reskinning a control.

Suggested sizes: 40–48 logical pixels for an inspector/EQ cap, at least a
44-pixel interaction area, and a 64-pixel cap for a spacious master section.
At 32 pixels and below, prefer the existing slider or a simplified flat dial.
Use dials for pan, EQ and selected effect parameters. Keep sliders for precise
long-travel level work and crossfading. BPM retains its exact numeric editor.

Interaction proposal:
  • Vertical drag changes value, independent of the pointer’s starting angle.
  • Shift-drag provides fine adjustment.
  • Double-click or an explicit reset command restores the parameter default.
  • Clicking the numeric readout opens exact entry with its current unit.
  • Focused arrow keys adjust; Home resets; retain appropriate parameter steps.
  • Mouse wheel affects only a focused dial, avoiding edits during scrolling.
  • Expose name, value, range and changes through the native accessibility API.
  • MIDI gestures, automation playback and mouse edits share one value source.

States: idle satin cap; hover brighter edge; active copper arc; keyboard focus
separate pale outline; disabled muted but readable; automation state explicit
through a small labeled indicator. Do not encode armed/write state through
copper alone. Distinct recording/error states remain easy to distinguish.

WORKSPACE APPLICATION — REVISED AFTER USER REVIEW
Use a three-column workspace below the global menu/toolbar:
  LEFT: full-height library, including search, cover art and track metadata.
        Library and Playlist tabs switch the list in the same column, keeping
        both existing workflows accessible. Sounds/Favorites in the artwork
        are illustrative, not claims of currently implemented features.
  CENTER: transport and automation above the arrangement; lane controls stay
          beside their waveforms and scroll vertically with their tracks.
  RIGHT: master output and the Clip/Effects/MIDI inspector, independently sized.
The library must no longer be a shallow panel above the arrangement. Switching
Library/Playlist must not unload the library or change the arrangement.

Place a vertical splitter between the library and central workspace, and a
second between the central workspace and right inspector. Each is a draggable
panel edge, not a parameter slider. Show an understated short grip at its
vertical center and a horizontal-resize cursor on hover. Use a 2-pixel visible
rail within an 8–10-pixel hit area. Hover/drag adds a restrained copper accent.
Resize live without obscuring controls or requiring a mouse release to preview.

Initial sizing proposal for a 1440-pixel-wide content area: library 300 pixels,
inspector 260, splitters 10 each, central workspace uses the remainder. Proposed
minimum widths: library 240, inspector 240, center 620 including lane controls.
These values require native verification before being treated as final. Both
splitters stop before any region falls below its usable minimum. Expanding one
side changes the central space and leaves the opposite side's chosen width
alone. Window resizing clamps the visible layout while retaining preferred
widths so they can return when the window grows again.

Double-click a divider to reset that side to its default width. Offer keyboard
focus and Left/Right arrow resizing, with a visible focus outline and accessible
names “Resize library” / “Resize inspector”. Remember preferred widths as local
workspace preferences, separate from the musical session and undo history.
Retain existing preference data and back it up when adding settings. Fully hiding
panels is a separate optional feature; ordinary resizing must not make a panel
unrecoverable. Long titles retain tooltips; narrow lists can scroll horizontally.

Give the timeline the strongest contrast and most visual space. Apply a common
4-pixel spacing rhythm, with 8/12/16-pixel gaps, restrained 4–6-pixel control
corners and no unnecessary surrounding boxes. The hexagon is a signature
control, not a general container shape for every button and panel.

Waveforms use clean filled geometry with a stable baseline. Make bar-one lines
stronger than beat lines. Keep automation curves readable over the waveform
through deliberate contrast and point handles. Use a thin near-white playhead
with a small copper head marker, without glow obscuring transients. Hover and
selection states must remain distinct. Keep track names/BPM/grid status visible.

MOTION
Use short 100–160 ms hover/press transitions where useful. The dial follows
input immediately; do not add inertia, bounce or delayed value animation.
Optional splash artwork can show two signals joining with a restrained copper
highlight. No continuous decorative animation in the working interface. Honor
reduced-motion preferences. Meter and waveform motion represents actual audio.

IMPLEMENTATION AND APPROVAL
First review the board’s material balance and the emblem/wordmark relationship.
Then build a small native JUCE sample: dial states, buttons, text fields, one
library row and a real timeline crop. Centralize semantic colors and drawing
rules in the owning LookAndFeel; use vector paths for marks and caps. Cache any
subtle texture rather than rendering a 3D asset per control. The supplied GLB
could support rendered branding artwork after visual inspection; it is not
needed for interactive dials. Do not introduce a 3D runtime solely for branding.

Before application rollout: inspect at 100%, 150% and 200% Windows scaling and
at the current minimum window size; check contrast and keyboard focus; verify
exact entry, reset, fine movement, MIDI and automation synchronization. Preserve
the existing workflow and parameter semantics. The generated concept board is
not evidence of native accessibility, production typography, or working controls.
